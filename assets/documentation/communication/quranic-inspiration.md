# Quranic Inspiration in OX-DX Communication

## Core boundary

**PHILOSOPHICAL INSPIRATION ≠ TECHNICAL AUTHORITY**

OX-DX is technically engineered and philosophically inspired.

Its Quranic dimension informs reflection around themes such as creation, order, measure, signs, knowledge, relation, observation, layers, meaning, and responsibility in making claims.

Those themes can shape questions and discipline. They do not replace engineering evidence.

## Preferred framing

Use language such as:

- “OX-DX is technically engineered and philosophically inspired.”
- “The Quranic dimension informs reflection around creation, order, measure, signs, relation, and observation.”
- “The engineering remains independently specified, testable, reviewable, and auditable.”
- “Philosophical inspiration gives the project questions; evidence decides technical claims.”

## Prohibited framing

Do not:

- fabricate Quranic quotations;
- invent Arabic text;
- imply divine validation of an implementation;
- describe the ontology as revelation;
- claim the 49 canonical levels are Quranic doctrine;
- cite the Qur'an as proof that a schema, algorithm, graph rule, or software result is technically correct;
- use religious authority to end an engineering disagreement.

## Tone

Be respectful and restrained.

Do not turn OX-DX documentation into preaching or religious merchandise language. Do not hide the inspiration either. State the boundary clearly once, then let the engineering stand on its own evidence.

## When sacred text is relevant

Only use an exact verse or Arabic text when it has been intentionally supplied and verified for that communication context. Brand-system generation must never invent sacred text.

## Engineering rule

For technical correctness, defer to:

- normative ontology specification;
- schemas and validation;
- engineering standards;
- implementation source;
- tests and CI;
- reproducible observations.

**Inspiration asks. Engineering proves only what evidence can support.**
