# OX-DX Spatial & Atmospheric Language

The atmosphere should make the viewer feel:

**contained · curious · focused**

Not overwhelmed.

## Density

Density changes according to task.

Broad context = lower sensory density.

Deep inspection = higher informational density but lower decorative density.

## Light

Light is evidence.

Use:
- localized signal;
- edge illumination;
- brief emergence;
- contrast to establish depth;
- brightness around a meaningful witness or event.

Avoid constant neon glow.

Bright light should indicate something.

## Contrast

Prefer deep dark/light separation with readable local hierarchy.

Atmosphere never lowers critical text/geometry contrast below accessible levels.

## Pace

Routine product:
- quiet;
- short;
- responsive.

Brand film/installations:
- slower;
- more space;
- larger silence intervals.

## Silence

Silence is atmospheric negative space.

Do not fill a quiet composition with hum merely because “tech” scenes often have one.

## Texture

Use deterministic:
- signal loss;
- trace erosion;
- boundary wear;
- compression scar;
- scan break.

Texture remains local and meaningful.

## Visual distance

Depth can be communicated by:
- density;
- contrast;
- persistence;
- scale;
- displacement.

Avoid fake volumetric hologram depth as the default.

## Spatial sound

Routine UI remains mono-compatible.

Immersive installations may use spatial relation, but no unique semantic information may depend on speaker position.
