# OX-DX Sensory Principles

## 1. EFFECTS ARE EVIDENCE

An effect should reveal a state, transition, event, relationship, or change.

If it communicates nothing, remove it.

## 2. RESOLVE, DON'T DECORATE

Visual and sonic behavior exists to clarify movement, relation, focus, or state.

It does not exist merely to look or sound futuristic.

## 3. SIGNAL OVER NOISE

Important events receive clearer sensory expression.

Routine interactions remain quiet.

Noise may represent interference or unresolved material, but noise is never automatically bad and signal is never automatically truth.

## 4. UNKNOWN CAN BE QUIET

Unknown is not failure by default.

Use incomplete continuity, deliberate void, reduced density, or silence instead of alarm.

## 5. OBSERVATION IS SUBTLE

Observation adds witness, trace, measurement, or context around a target.

It does not visually or sonically take over the target.

## 6. VERIFICATION SETTLES

Verification should feel stable, settled, and precise.

Avoid confetti, explosions, fanfare, or broad green flashes.

## 7. ERROR HAS WEIGHT

Error creates local friction:
- interruption;
- offset;
- drop;
- short low-weight transient;
- clear text.

It should not trigger theatrical panic.

## 8. SILENCE IS PART OF THE SYSTEM

No sound, no motion, or minimal visual response can be the correct state.

Silence is not missing polish.

It is restraint.

## 9. ONE EVENT, ONE REASON

Every visual/audio/motion cue must be traceable to a product event or brand moment.

## 10. ACCESSIBILITY OVERRIDES ATMOSPHERE

If an effect reduces comprehension, focus, contrast, comfort, or control, reduce or remove it.
